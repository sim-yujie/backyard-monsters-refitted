The current phone spends most of its height reporting production before letting you produce anything. Shrink that reporting, preserve the monster artwork, and anchor Add. Both directions below use the same geometry for steel/gold Modern and parchment/wood/ribbon Classic. Every interactive target is at least 44×44 px; all icons are painted assets.

## X — Monster book with a production seal

**The catalogue owns the window. One large illustrated seal summarizes production; tapping it opens a dedicated inspection page.**

The production seal is a painted hatchery with **5/5**, a queue portrait cluster with **10/10**, and an hourglass with **12h51m**. Housing shows a house, **2,080/2,160**, and a narrow forecast segment. No hatchery cards, empty boxes, explanatory paragraph or standing Clear all button.

Five 52×52 mix medallions show a three-monster collage and slot number. The unused fifth shows a painted plus. Four columns of 82×92 monster cards show portrait and goo cost; names appear only for the selected monster.

The bottom 108 px contains a 44 px portrait/name row and controls: minus, count, plus, Max; underneath, a full-width 44 px gold **Add** button. Remove +5/+10; holding plus repeats.

**844×390:** Same 48 px title and 44 px tabs. Remaining interior: 200 px production/mix block beside a 612 px catalogue. The catalogue’s bottom 96 px holds controls; its grid scrolls above. No workspace switch.

**Desktop:** A broad illustrated catalogue with a fixed 260 px production instrument beside it. Controls remain under the catalogue. Tapping the instrument opens inspection in the existing window body, never a drawer.

**Flow**
1. Choose monster → stepper → Add.
2. Tap a saved medallion: immediately fill the queue and pay goo; briefly animate portraits travelling into the seal.
3. Tap the empty medallion: save current queue. Hold a saved one to inspect, replace or erase.
4. Tap production seal: inspect individual hatcheries and ten stacks; remove stacks or use speed-up/clear controls. Painted back arrow restores catalogue.

**Resting words:** **10**: Monsters; six tab labels; Pokey; Max; Add. Mixes have no names. Numbers and units excluded.

**Main risk:** Individual queue contents require another tap. The seal must visibly signal stalled housing and a full queue.

Each queue socket is 64×48: portrait plus quantity, no name. Empty sockets are shallow dark recesses, not labelled boxes. Each hatchery is a 44 px portrait target with a progress arc; blocked hatcheries receive a painted house badge. Tapping reveals its exact timer.

The housing strip shows current/capacity and forecast numerically. Four-column monster cards are 82×88. The bottom controls include selected portrait/name, total goo and completion time, then stepper and Add. Ten full stacks, five busy hatcheries and four saved mixes all fit.

**844×390:** After title/tabs, split the body into a 340 px workbench and 472 px catalogue. Queue, hatcheries and recipes occupy the workbench; catalogue controls stay pinned beneath its scrolling grid.

**Desktop:** One horizontal ten-socket rack, catalogue beneath, recipe tokens and selection controls permanently along the bottom.

**Flow**
1. Choose → stepper → Add; portraits animate into sockets.
2. Tap a socket to replace the selection controls temporarily with quantity removal controls.
3. Recipe tokens load/save exactly as X.

**Resting words:** **10**, same count as X.

**Main risk:** More visual density; socket artwork needs restrained borders.

## Shared yard navigation and locks

The 48 px yard strip always shows a painted home-return target, flag, immutable short yard ID and coordinates. Remote mode adds a gold inner border; Add repeats that flag. Full names appear in inspection.

Tap the flag to open a full-body territory atlas: search names/coordinates, favourites, clustered map markers. Results become selectable map cards, not a permanent yard rail. Scale through search and clustering to hundreds.

Below L2, replace recipe tokens with one locked collage marked **2**. Below L3, the atlas flag bears a lock and **3**.

## My pick

**X.** It gives monsters the most space and removes the wall of boxes.
Production inspection remains available without governing the resting layout.
Build Y only if seeing every queued stack continuously is essential.
