# Bot Neighbours for Map Room 1 — Design

A design document for issue #233. The owner's request, as settled on 2026-10-03: make sure new
players always have yards to attack, by filling Map Room 1 neighbour lists with computer-run
players ("bots") that look and behave like real players.

All citations are `path:line` or `path` relative to the repository root. Claims about current
behaviour cite the server, the web client or the Flash client (`client/scripts`); anything new is
marked as a proposal. Numbers that have not been playtested are marked `[PLACEHOLDER]`.

## 1. Goals

- **A new player always has something to attack.** Their Map Room 1 neighbour list is never
  empty or full of protected yards, from their first day.
- **Bots are players as far as anyone can tell.** Same names, avatars, levels, yards, loot,
  defences, protection and revenge as a real player of that level would have.
- **Real players first.** Bots only take the neighbour places real players leave empty.
- **Cheap and safe to run live.** 500 rows of ordinary users and saves, one minute sweep, no new
  services, one switch to turn it all off.

Non-goals: Map Room 2 and 3 (untouched), outposts, alliances, chat, leaderboards, bots attacking
first, bots attacking each other.

## 2. The owner's decisions

These are settled (2026-10-03) and this document does not re-open them. Where the code makes one
costly, section 12 says so.

1. **Purpose:** new players always have yards to attack. Live game, not dev-only.
2. **Map Room 1 only.** Map Room 2 and 3 untouched.
3. **Bots look exactly like real players:** real-looking names, no bot tag anywhere a player can
   see.
4. **500 bots in total** (global; Map Room 1 has no worlds), spread evenly over levels 1-40.
5. **Neighbour lists:** up to 25 neighbours within ±7 levels, cached 2 weeks, 30-minute retry under
   10. Real players get places first; bots fill the rest. The 150-most-recent-saves limit must be
   handled, because bots never log in.
6. **Yards** are generated by a program for the bot's level, inside the same per-Town-Hall limits
   a real player has, with randomness in placement so they look player-made.
7. **Growth:** bots slowly level up like a player upgrading; a bot that outgrows its level slot is
   swapped for a fresh low-level bot, keeping the even 1-40 spread and the total at 500.
8. **Loot:** about what a real player of that level has: storage partly full, harvesters fill over
   time, refilled after attacks.
9. **Defence:** monsters in Monster Bunkers (housed monsters do not defend) plus a champion in its
   cage once the bot is high enough, like a real player of that level.
10. **After an attack:** normal post-attack protection, then the bot repairs itself over a few
    hours (as a player pressing Repair).
11. **Revenge only.** After each attack on a bot, about 1 in 3 chance the bot attacks that player
    back within 1-24 hours, never while the player is protected (new-player protection included).
    Reuse Map Room 1's `attacksfrom` / `attacksto` / `retaliatecount`.
12. **Revenge army:** what a real player at the bot's level could field: monsters unlocked by then,
    up to housing capacity, plus its champion if it has one.
13. **Revenge is fought on the server** with the normal combat engine while the player is away:
    real loot and damage, post-attack protection, a defence report in the mail, plus a replay where
    the game already supports one.
14. **Messages** to bots go unanswered; alliance and friend invites are declined after a random
    few hours; bots never chat.
15. **Home yard only:** no outposts, no alliances, not on leaderboards.
16. **The 2,500 seeded Map Room 2 dev players stay.** Fix the seed script so it can never hand out
    the `DEV_SANDBOX` maxed yard.

The owner's answers to this design's questions (2026-10-03):

17. **Growth is a conveyor:** each bot climbs through all the levels at about **3 days per level**,
    retires once it passes level 40, and a fresh level 1 bot takes its place (section 4.4).
18. **Truce requests** to a bot are declined after a random **2-8 hours**.
19. **Revenge caps:** at most **2 revenge attacks on one player per 24 hours**, **1 per bot-player
    pair per 24 hours**; a revenge that cannot run gives up **72 hours** after the attack that
    triggered it.
20. **Only real players seen in the last 30 days** take neighbour places, read from a new
    `user.last_seen_at` column set by the owner's own yard load.
21. **Defence reports for every Map Room 1 main-yard defence** (real or bot attacker): a mail notice
    plus the "While you were away" toast, in v1. A **Watch replay for every such defence** comes in
    v1.1 (WP13).
22. **Bots appear online now and then,** about 2 minutes at a time, which blocks attacks on them for
    that minute as for a real player.
23. **No early re-search when a player's level changes:** the 2-week cache stays as it is. (The
    separate "fewer than 5 attackable" retry in section 4.3 is kept: it reacts to bots under
    protection, not to the player's level.)

## 3. What already exists

### 3.1 Map Room 1 neighbours

- **The search** (`server/src/services/maproom/v1/findOverworldNeighbours.ts`): level is
  `calculateBaseLevel(points, basevalue)` (`:24`), the window is ±7 (`:25-27`), and the candidates
  are the **150 most recently updated** Map Room 1 main saves (`:29-41`, `limit: 150`,
  `orderBy: lastupdateAt DESC`), filtered in memory and cut at 25 (`:46-55`). A player outside
  those 150 rows is never found, bot or not: this is a bug for real players too.
- **The cache** lives in `maproom.neighbors` (one row per player). It is re-searched after 2
  weeks (`getNeighbours.ts:20`), or after 30 minutes while it holds fewer than 10
  (`getNeighbours.ts:23`, `:170-176`). The Map Room 1 re-search **replaces** the list and loses the
  attack counters (`getNeighbours.ts:147-153`); the Inferno re-search keeps them
  (`getNeighbours.ts:73-85`).
- **Every read refreshes live fields** (`updateNeighbourData.ts:48-152`): it drops any neighbour
  whose save is gone or is no longer on Map Room 1 (`:52`, `:89`), and sets protection
  (`:91`, `:109-118`, the 7-day new-player protection told apart at `:101-103`), the level
  (`:122`), the name and avatar (`:127-132`), truces (`:134-144`) and `saved`, the last-seen time
  from Redis (`:123`).
- **A neighbour entry** (`createNeighbourData.ts:27-57`) carries `userid`, `baseid`, `username`,
  `pic`, `level`, `attacksto`, `attacksfrom`, `retaliatecount`, `attacksTodayCount`, `friend`,
  `attackpermitted` and truce fields. The web reads it in `web/src/game/maproom1/mr1Model.ts:153-176`.
- **Attack counters** are written when an attack starts (`registerAttacker.ts:19-91`, called from
  `baseModeAttack.ts:297`): the attacker goes to the top of the defender's list with
  `attacksfrom + 1` (`:37-58`), the defender's entry in the attacker's list gets `attacksto + 1`
  (`:61-66`), and 10 attacks on one neighbour in a day drops the pair from each other's lists
  (`:68-85`). **`retaliatecount` is never written** by the server.
- **Flash used them for colour and labels only:** a red pin when `attacksfrom > attacksto`
  (`client/scripts/com/monsters/maproom/ForeignBase.as:72-83`), "Hostile" when `attacksfrom > 1`
  (`PlayerHandler.as:43-46`), and a "Vengeance" status showing `retaliatecount` when
  `attackpermitted == 4` (`PlayerHandler.as:86-89`). The server has the level-restriction and
  vengeance permissions only as a TODO (`updateNeighbourData.ts:105-108`), so any Map Room 1 player
  can attack any other today.

### 3.2 Attacking a Map Room 1 player

- **The attack load** (`server/src/controllers/base/load/modes/baseModeAttack.ts:71-302`) takes
  any Map Room 1 main yard by base id (`:86-87`) and refuses before writing anything when the
  target is protected (`:96`), already under attack (`:98`), seen in the last 60 seconds (`:100-103`)
  or in a truce with the attacker (`:105-107`). Range is not checked on Map Room 1
  (`validateRange.ts:55-57`). It catches both armies up (`:156`), drops the attacker's own
  protection (`:175-177`), mints the attack session (`:268-287`), and writes the attack counters
  and an `attack_logs` row (`:290-299`).
- **"Online" is a Redis key** `last-seen:main:<userid>` with a 120-second life, set by the owner's
  base load (`baseLoad.ts:104`), base save (`baseSave.ts:591`) and the Flash poll
  (`updateSaved.ts:49`). Flash shows "online" when it is under 62 seconds old
  (`PlayerHandler.as:32-35`). **The web client never polls** (no `updatesaved` call in `web/src`),
  so a web player sitting idle in their yard reads as offline after a minute and can be attacked.
- **Landing.** A battle lands either from the attacker's final save (`baseSave.ts`) or from a
  checkpoint through the finaliser (`finaliseAttack.ts:163-388`). The finaliser replays in a
  worker, spends the flung monsters (`:249`), banks the loot up to the attacker's storage
  (`:282`), writes the defender's health, damage, garrisons, champion and loot (`:287-316`),
  grants post-attack protection (`:319`) and tells an outpost's owner (`:321-329`).
  `landCheckpointedAttack` (`:403`) is the same path for a checkpoint the server wrote itself.
- **Auto-attack already lands a server-made battle** (#221,
  `server/src/services/base/autoAttack/autoAttack.ts:258-393`): it mints the attack with
  `baseModeAttack`, writes a checkpoint from a plan under a fresh seed, takes a replay-worker slot
  (`reserveReplaySlot`, `combat/replayRunner.ts:89`) and lands it through
  `landCheckpointedAttack`. It keeps the battle in Redis for **Watch**, a playback of the server's
  battle in the attack scene (`keepReplay`; `web/src/ui/attack/WatchPanels.ts`). **A revenge
  attack is the same operation with a bot as the attacker and a player's yard as the target.**

### 3.3 Protection

`server/src/services/maproom/v2/damageProtection.ts`:

- Attacking anyone ends your protection (`:42-48`).
- A main yard gets 1 hour after 4 attacks in an hour (`:71-72`) and **36 hours once an attack
  leaves it at 50% damage or more** (`:74-77`). `protectAfterAttack` applies this when an attack
  lands (`:167-183`).
- New accounts get 7 days (`server/src/game-data/getDefaultBaseData.ts:37`, `:46`).

### 3.4 Levels, building limits and yard rules

- **Level** = the highest `i + 1` with `points + basevalue >= experiencePoints[i]`
  (`services/base/calculateBaseLevel.ts:11-20`, `game-data/stats/experiencePoints.ts`). Level 40
  starts at 976,208,337 and level 41 at 1,366,691,671.
- **Points** come from finished builds (`time / 2 + resources / 10`) and upgrades
  (`(time + resources) / 3`) (`services/yardplanner/costs.ts:253-269`, awarded in
  `catchUpBuildings.ts`). **Base value** is a tenth of every finished building's last step
  (`economy/resourceBudget.ts:369-388`), raised by `syncBaseValue` (`yard/derivedLevels.ts:123-131`).
- **A fully built yard reaches level 42.** Summing every type at its Town Hall 10 quantity and top
  level (scratch run of `baseValueOf` and the two points formulas) gives base value ≈ 247 M plus
  points ≈ 2.13 B. So level 40 is a nearly maxed yard and the 1-40 range is reachable by the rules.
- **Limits:** `quantity[hall]` per type per Town Hall level, and each step's `re` prerequisites
  (`game-data/buildingCosts.ts:25-26`, `:45-52`). Examples: Monster Bunker from Town Hall 3
  (`:297-304`), Champion Cage from Town Hall 4 (`:675-677`), Map Room level 2 needs Town Hall 6
  (`:209-212`).
- **The server already decides every building action as a pure function:** `planBuild`
  (`yard/build.ts:359`, gates at `:76-103`), `planUpgradeAction` (`yard/upgrade.ts:143`),
  `planInstantBuild` / `planInstantUpgrade`, `planRepair` (`yard/repair.ts:158`),
  `planBunkerFill`, `planLockerStart`, `planChampionRaise` (`yard/champion.ts:218`). Placement
  uses `layoutGeometry.ts` (`YARD_SIZES` `:25-39`, `rectOf` `:68`, `overlaps` `:74`,
  `withinBounds` `:92`).
- **`catchUpYard(save, now)`** (`yard/catchUp.ts:45-160`) is one pure function that advances a
  yard: builds finish and award points, repairs heal, harvesters fill their buffers, champions heal
  and starve, `basevalue` is synced. `catchUpLockedYard` (`controllers/yard/yardAction.ts:375-389`)
  runs it under the row lock, and the attack load runs it on the defender (`yard/armies.ts:106`).
  **This is how a bot's harvesters fill and repairs heal without a client.**
- **A level 2 Map Room moves a yard to Map Room 2** (`yard/mapRoom.ts:279-282`, run by
  `catchUpLockedYard`). Bots must never upgrade their Map Room.

### 3.5 Defence and armies

- Bunker contents are `buildingdata[id].m` (`yard/bunker.ts:103`), capacity by level
  (`:96`), bunkerable monsters `C1`-`C13`, `C17` (`:53-55`).
- The defence a battle fights is the bunker garrisons plus the caged champion at the owner's
  academy levels (`services/base/combat/defenderForces.ts:15-20`, `defenderForcesOf`
  `game-rules/combat/defence.ts:80`). Housed monsters do not defend.
- Housing capacity: `housingCapacity` (`yard/housing.ts:38`). Unlocks: `unlockGate`, by Monster
  Locker level (`yard/locker.ts:176-204`). Champions starve one feed per catch-up once 24 hours
  hungry (`yard/catchUpChampions.ts`, file comment).

### 3.6 Mail, invites, chat, leaderboards

- **System notices** come from `userid` 0 (`services/mail/systemSender.ts`), written by
  `writeOutpostNotice` and handed to the next own-yard load as "While you were away" toasts
  (`services/maproom/v2/outpostNotices.ts:105-158`, `baseLoad.ts:198`). **A main yard that is
  attacked gets no notice today**, from a bot or a player.
- **Truces** (#203): 7 days, a 2-day wait after a rejection (`services/mail/truceRules.ts:23-29`),
  answered through `handleTruceResponse` (`services/mail/handleTruceResponse.ts:25`). The Map Room
  1 target card has no Truce or Message button yet (`web/src/ui/maproom1/TargetCard.ts:33`), so
  today a truce or message can reach a Map Room 1 player only from an existing mail thread.
- **Alliances** need a world: inviting a player without one is refused
  (`controllers/alliance/inviteUser.ts:51`), and suggested members are drawn from world players
  only (`services/alliance/suggestedMembers.ts:48`). A Map Room 1 bot has no world, so it can never
  be invited. **There is no friend system on the server.**
- **Leaderboards** exist for Map Room 2 and 3 only, built from world map cells
  (`controllers/leaderboards/getLeaderboards.ts:54`). Bots have no cells.
- **Chat** is a separate WebSocket gateway (`server/src/chat/`); a bot never connects.

### 3.7 Timed work on the server

- One in-process sweep exists: the attack finaliser, every 60 seconds (`finaliseAttack.ts:125`,
  `:508-522`), started in `server.ts:101` unless `ATTACK_FINALISER_SWEEP=off`.
- Periodic scripts run from cron and claim their period in `bym.job_run`
  (`scripts/monthly-shiny.ts`, migration `20260912_CreateJobRunTable.ts`).
- Nothing yet runs work due at a future time per row. Section 6 adds that.

### 3.8 The seed hazard (decision 16)

All three seed scripts copy `devConfig.devSandbox` into `user.sandbox_start`
(`database/seeds/seed_maproom2.ts:57`, `seed_maproom3.ts:58`, `seed_alliances.ts:76`), and
`getDefaultBaseData` then hands out the maxed sandbox yard (`game-data/getDefaultBaseData.ts:30`).
`server/.env` has `DEV_SANDBOX=true`, so the next seed run makes 2,500 maxed yards. The seeded
saves are `mapversion` 1 (checked read-only: 2,500 main saves with `mapversion = 1` and a world),
so they are Map Room 1 neighbour candidates in dev.

## 4. How it works

### 4.1 What a bot is

A bot is an ordinary `user` row and an ordinary Map Room 1 main `save`, plus one row in a new
`bot` table that only the server reads (section 5). Nothing on the user or save says "bot".

- **Name:** generated from word lists in the shapes real players use ("MossyGoblin", "kai_builds",
  "Pebble77", "xXSnapperXx"), filtered by the same bad-word filter as player names
  (`services/alliance/textFilter.ts`), unique against `user.username`.
- **Avatar:** one of the twelve player avatars (`game-data/avatars.ts`), or none, in the ratio
  real players show.
- **Account:** email `bot+<uuid>@bymr.invalid`, a random 64-byte password hash. Login and password
  reset refuse users in the `bot` table (a one-line guard each), so the account cannot be entered.
- **Age:** `createtime` is back-dated to look as old as its level (about its level times the days
  per level, with jitter), so 500 yards do not share one creation minute.
- **Protection:** none at creation (a new-player protection would make it unattackable for a week).
- **Map Room:** `mapversion` 1, no world, Map Room building at level 1 for life.

### 4.2 Yard generator

`services/bots/yardGenerator.ts` (proposal), pure and seeded (the server already depends on
`alea`). Input: the bot's seed and a target points value. Output: every save slice a yard needs
(`buildingdata`, `resources`, `lockerdata`, `academy`, `monsters`, `champion`, `storedata`,
`points`, `basevalue`).

It works the way a player does, so the limits hold by construction:

1. **Progression.** A build order simulated from the starter base
   (`yard/starterBase.ts:48`): at each step, pick the next action from what the rules allow now,
   weighted by the bot's persona (economy-first, tower-first, army-first). An action is legal only
   if the server's own gate passes: `quantity[hall]`, `re` prerequisites and maximum level, the
   same checks `planBuild` and `planUpgradeAction` make. Each action adds its points
   (`pointsForBuild` / `pointsForUpgrade`). Stop when points plus base value reach the target.
   The Map Room never goes past level 1.
2. **Placement.** Each new building gets a spot from zones around the Town Hall, after the wiki's
   [Base Defense Guide](https://backyardmonsters.fandom.com/wiki/Base_Defense_Guide), which most
   players follow (owner reviews, issues #250 and #252): the Town Hall dead centre with four silos
   in a small walled core that a Town Hall 3 yard's walls close (the guide's Town Hall and Silo
   Death Trap), a 3 x 3 grid of walled compartments with the core as its middle, holding the
   towers with the harvesters in front of them and inside their range, defences spread so no side
   is weak, each kind of harvester spread out, the general and monster buildings round the edge
   (outside the grid once the plot has room: the guide's Never Ending Chain). Walls close the grid
   one compartment at a time, the core first, and a compartment is only started when the walls
   the bot's Town Hall will stand can close it, so at most one is ever being built; walls it cannot
   use yet double the core, a side at a time. The grid and that ring hold every wall a Town Hall
   10 allows, so no wall stands on its own. Only the core has openings, a two-cell hallway on each
   long side (and the ring cells in front), which traps fill first; nothing stands in a wall line
   or in front of a hallway. Further traps go between harvesters and silos and by towers. The base
   is compact, as real players build: compartments only a row or two of buildings deep, every
   building packed hard by its neighbours and the walls and as far in as its zone allows, the
   compartments filled edge to edge before anything spills out, and from Town Hall 4 the general
   and monster buildings packed round the outside of the grid. Decorations are rare: one, on about 15% of bots `[PLACEHOLDER]`. Every
   spot is jittered, checked with `rectOf` / `overlaps` / `withinBounds` (`layoutGeometry.ts`), and
   the plot grows with the yard expansions a player of that level usually owns (`storedata.ENL.q`,
   up to 6 like the sandbox yard, `utils/sandbox/overworldYard.ts:3864`; one at about levels 10,
   14, 19, 24, 29 and 34 `[PLACEHOLDER]`, each bot shifted by up to 2 levels). The progression
   builds walls and traps as such a player does: from Town Hall 3 it keeps 90-100% of the wall
   allowance and 70-100% of the trap allowance standing `[PLACEHOLDER]`, and it upgrades its walls
   (real costs, Town Hall gates and points) towards an average level per Town Hall, mostly wood at
   Town Hall 3, stone by Town Hall 4-5, level 4-5 by Town Hall 10 `[PLACEHOLDER]`.
3. **Army and defence.** Unlocked monsters: those the Locker level allows
   (`yard/locker.ts:176-204`), less a random one or two of the newest. Academy levels by level band.
   Bunkers filled to 70-100% of capacity with bunkerable unlocked monsters (`yard/bunker.ts:53`,
   `:96`); Housing filled to 80-100% of `housingCapacity`. A champion in the cage once there is a
   cage (Town Hall 4), at a champion level that grows with the bot's level `[PLACEHOLDER]` table in
   WP4.
4. **Loot.** Each resource set to a random 25-70% of the storage cap `[PLACEHOLDER]`; harvester
   buffers start part full and then fill by the normal catch-up.

The target points value is drawn uniformly inside the level's band
(`experiencePoints[L-1]` to `experiencePoints[L]`), so two bots of one level are not the same
size. Because the progression is seeded and deterministic, "the yard at points P" is a pure
function of (seed, P): growth (4.4) is the same progression run a little further.

### 4.3 Neighbour lists (decision 5)

`findOverworldNeighbours` is rewritten in two steps:

1. **Real players.** One query over Map Room 1 main saves that are not bots and whose owner was
   seen in the last 30 days (`user.last_seen_at`, decision 20), filtered in SQL by
   the level window turned into an empire-points range
   (`(points::numeric + basevalue::numeric) >= experiencePoints[min-1] AND < experiencePoints[max]`),
   newest first, limit 25. This also keeps quit accounts and the 2,500 seeded dev players (which
   never log in) out of the real-player places. No 150-row cut, so every real player in range can be found. It runs at
   most once per player per cache period, so a scan is affordable; an expression index is noted in
   WP3 if the live table needs it.
2. **Bots fill the rest.** `25 − real` bots from the `bot` table (`state = 'active'`,
   `level BETWEEN min AND max`), not already in the list, preferring bots that are attackable now
   (not protected, not under attack), shuffled and spread across the levels in range.

The cached list stores bot entries exactly as real ones (`createNeighbourData`), and the whole
list is shuffled before it is cached, so the order (real players first, bots after) gives nothing
away. As built (WP3): "newest first" in step 1 is most recently seen (`last_seen_at`), and with
`BOTS_FILL` off step 2 is skipped, so the list holds real players only. On re-search the
attack counters of neighbours that stay are carried over, as the Inferno path already does
(`getNeighbours.ts:73-85`); this also fixes the counters reset for real players.

**Keeping lists useful.** A bot attacked hard sits under 36-hour protection, so a two-week list
can go stale. So the list is also re-searched after the 30-minute retry when fewer than 5 entries
are attackable right now. Real players keep their places (step 1 runs first); only the bot picks
change. There is **no** early re-search when the player's own level changes (decision 23): that
is a different trigger, and the 2-week cache stays as it is.

### 4.4 Growth and the even spread (decision 7)

Decision 17: **a conveyor**. Each bot climbs through the levels at the same average pace, `T` = 3
days per level (so a bot lives about four months). A bot that passes level 40 has outgrown the
range: it retires and a fresh level 1 bot is created in its place. With an equal pace per level,
the 1-40 spread stays even on its own; a daily rebalance corrects drift.

- **Grow job** (every 2-6 hours per bot, jittered): the bot's target points move linearly from the
  bottom of its level band to the top over `T` days. The job runs the bot's own progression forward
  until its points reach the target, through the real pure gates (4.2), then `catchUpYard`, all
  under the row lock as `catchUpLockedYard` does. It leaves the last upgrade running on a real
  timer (capped so it finishes inside the pace), so an attacker sees a busy worker as on a real
  yard. Resources the bot "spends" are not charged: loot is kept in its band by 4.6 instead,
  because a bot has no player farming for it.
- **Retirement** = the bot "moves to Map Room 2": its save gets `mapversion` 2 with no world, and
  its `bot.state` becomes `retired`. Every neighbour list drops it on the next read
  (`updateNeighbourData.ts:52`, the same path a real player who upgrades off Map Room 1 takes); it
  has no map cell, so nobody sees it anywhere. Its rows stay, so mail threads and attack logs keep
  their names. Retired bots with no mail older than 90 days `[PLACEHOLDER]` may be deleted.
- **Rebalance** (daily, claimed through `bym.job_run`, section 6): any level more than 2 bots
  over its share has its newest arrivals' growth slowed; any level more than 2 under has its
  arrivals sped up; the total is topped back to 500 at level 1.

### 4.5 After a player attacks a bot (decisions 10, 11)

A new shared hook, `afterYardDefended`, runs where both landings already tell an outpost's owner:
`baseSave.ts:557-567` and `finaliseAttack.ts:320-329`. When the defender is a bot and the attacker
is a real player:

1. **Protection** is the normal `protectAfterAttack`, already run by both paths.
2. **Repair:** schedule a `repair` job at a random 1-4 hours `[PLACEHOLDER]` after the attack. It
   runs `planRepair({ all: true })` under the row lock (the player's Repair button); the catch-up
   heals at the normal rates (an hour at most per building, `yard/catchUpRepairs.ts`), so the yard
   is whole a few hours after the attack.
3. **Refill:** with the repair, bunkers back to their band, Housing refilled, resources back to the
   band (4.6), the champion fed and healed.
4. **Revenge roll:** with chance 1/3 `[PLACEHOLDER]`, schedule a `revenge` job against this attacker
   at a uniform 1-24 hours, subject to the caps in 7.2.

### 4.6 Loot (decision 8)

- **Harvesters** fill on their own by the normal catch-up (`catchUpHarvesters.ts`), which runs on
  every attack load and every bot job.
- **Storage** is held in a band: on creation, after each repair and on each grow job, any resource
  outside 25-70% of its cap `[PLACEHOLDER]` is moved to a random point inside it. Loot from a
  revenge attack banks into the bot as for any attacker (`bankAttackLoot`), then the band applies.

### 4.7 Revenge attack (decisions 11-13)

The `revenge` job, when due:

1. **Checks** (refuse or postpone, nothing written): the bot is active and not under attack; the
   target's main save is still on Map Room 1; no truce between them; the player is **not
   protected** (`save.protected > now`, which covers new-player, damage and bought protection);
   the player is **not online** (the last-seen key, which needs the web presence ping, 8.1).
   Online or under attack: retry in 15-60 minutes. Protected: retry when the protection ends plus
   1-6 hours. Every retry gives up 72 hours after the attack that triggered it (decision 19).
2. **Army:** the bot's housed monsters (kept full by the refill) and its caged champion if alive.
   The brain does not top Housing up for the attack; it fights with what it has, as a player
   would.
3. **Plan:** `services/bots/revengePlan.ts` (pure): one to three drop points on the yard edge facing
   the densest buildings, the army split into drops no bigger than the Flinger payload
   (`flingerPayload`, `game-rules/combat/engine.ts:791`), 2-8 seconds apart, the champion with the
   first drop. The battle runs to its natural end; no bombs, siege or retreat. (Flash's wild
   monster raids spawn the same way, on a ring outside the yard, `client/scripts/WMATTACK.as`
   `SpawnWave`.)
4. **Run** exactly as auto-attack does (`autoAttack.ts:258-393`): take `reserveReplaySlot` (busy:
   retry in 5 minutes), `baseModeAttack({ user: bot, baseid: player's base, mapversion: V1,
   attackerLevel })`, write a checkpoint with a fresh seed, `landCheckpointedAttack(...,
   { left: false, recordPlan: false })`.
5. **Everything a real attack does then happens by itself:** the bot's protection ends; the attack
   counters move (`registerAttacker`: the bot shows as "Hostile" on the player's map once
   `attacksfrom > 1`); `attack_logs` gets a row; the player's yard takes real damage and loses real
   loot; housed monsters die with fallen Housing; the player gets post-attack protection; the
   defence notice (4.8) goes out. `retaliatecount` on the player's entry for the bot goes up by one
   (decision 11; nothing reads it on the web today).

Cost: one replay, about 0.2-1.1 s in a worker on a full player yard (`docs/design/auto-attack.md`
§6.3); a few hundred a day at most.

As built (WP11, `services/bots/revenge.ts` for the checks, `services/bots/revengeRun.ts` for the
attack, run by the sweep): the job is claimed only with `BOTS_BRAIN` and `BOTS_REVENGE` both on; with
`BOTS_REVENGE` off every pass deletes the booked revenge jobs. Details the text above leaves open:

- **The checks run in this order**, all read fresh on the job's transaction: past the give-up time,
  bot not active, player off Map Room 1, **this bot already attacked the player since the attack that
  booked the job** (its `attack_logs` row; so a crash between the attack and the job's delete never
  attacks twice), truce: **cancel**. Bot under attack, player online or under attack: 15-60 minutes.
  Protected: protection end + 1-6 hours. A truce cancels rather than waits (it lasts 7 days, past
  the 72-hour give-up). Any wait that would end at or past the give-up time cancels instead.
- **The caps at run time** count bots' attacks on the player that have happened (`attack_logs`), not
  booked jobs, so revenges that run keep both caps however retries moved them. A full cap waits until
  the window frees, plus 15-60 minutes.
- **"Online"** is a last-seen mark in the key's whole life (120 s), stricter than the attack load's
  60 s.
- **The per-bot lock** is the bot's `bym.bot` row, taken `FOR NO KEY UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` for the job's
  transaction (another server holding it: retry in 5 minutes); a `grow` waits on it.
- **The plan is made before the attack is minted**, so "nothing to send" (`planRevenge` null) cancels
  with nothing written. No replay slot: retry in 5 minutes. The attack load refusing at the last
  moment (a check raced): 15-60 minutes.
- **`retaliatecount` + 1** is written right after the attack load, before the landing, so a revenge
  whose replay runs past its deadline (it lands later from its checkpoint through the attack
  finaliser's sweep) still counts. Such a late landing goes through the finaliser's default, so its
  battle report reads "left the attack", as for any attack landed that way.

### 4.8 Defence report and replay (decision 13)

- **Report:** `afterYardDefended` writes a system notice for **every Map Room 1 main-yard defence**,
  real or bot attacker, in `writeOutpostNotice`'s form: "Kai_Builds attacked your yard", the damage
  it was left at, what was looted, housed monsters lost. The next own-yard load shows it in the "While
  you were away" toast, as outpost notices are (`takeOutpostNotices`). It must cover real attacks
  too, or a report would itself say "this was a bot" (decision 3).
- **Replay:** the game has a playback mode for a server-fought battle (auto-attack's Watch), but no
  defender replay of any kind. For the same reason as the report, a replay offered only for bot
  revenge would give bots away. Decision 21: a v1.1 "Watch" button on the defence notice for every
  Map Room 1 main-yard defence, storing seed, log and the yard as the battle found it, as
  `keepReplay` does (WP13).

### 4.9 Messages, invites, chat (decision 14)

- **Messages** to a bot are stored as any message and never answered. Nothing to build.
- **Truce requests** to a bot: schedule a `declineTruce` job at a random 2-8 hours (decision 18)
  that answers through `handleTruceResponse` with Flash's "I reject your truce."
- **Alliance invites** cannot reach a bot (no world, `inviteUser.ts:51`); the same decline job is
  wired in case that changes. **Friend invites** do not exist on the server.
- **Chat:** a bot never connects. Nothing to build.
- **Presence:** each grow job marks the bot online for 2 minutes (`last-seen:main:<bot>`, as a real
  load does), so bots are sometimes seen online and, for that minute, cannot be attacked, like a
  player (decision 22).

As built (WP12, `services/bots/truceDecline.ts`): both routes that make a request (`requesttruce` and
`sendmessage` `trucerequest`) call `bookTruceDecline` once the truce row exists; for a bot it books a
`declineTruce` job (`payload.truce`, `target_userid` the proposer) due a uniform 2-8 hours later, and
the route answers exactly as for a player. The sweep (`BOTS_BRAIN`) runs it as a player pressing Reject
in the web mailbox: the bot opens the thread (what it holds for the bot is read), the truce is answered
by `answerTruce` (`services/mail/truceTimes.ts`, the rule `handleTruceResponse` uses, split out so the
sweep need not import `server.js`), and a `trucereject` reply is written from the bot with the thread's
subject and "I reject your truce.", the thread's count and last message moved and both unread counts
recounted, as `sendMessage` does. A request no longer open when the job runs (lapsed or answered), or
between players who have blocked each other since (a player could not answer it either), is left alone.
Alliance invitations are **not** wired: a bot has no world, so `inviteUser` refuses it, and an
invitation to move needs a Map Room 2 player; nothing reaches a bot to decline. A retired bot's pending
decline goes with its other jobs, and the request lapses unanswered after 7 days.

### 4.10 Seed fix (decision 16)

The three seed scripts set `sandbox_start: false` always; `getDefaultBaseData` additionally refuses
the sandbox yard to any account in the `bot` table. A test seeds one user with `DEV_SANDBOX=true`
and checks the yard is the starter yard.

## 5. Data model and migrations

One migration, `server/src/database/migrations/2026XXXX_CreateBotTables.ts`, in the existing
`Migration` + `addSql` style (e.g. `20261001_CreateAttackPlanTable.ts`):

```sql
CREATE TABLE bym.bot (
  userid        integer PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES bym."user"(userid),
  seed          bigint      NOT NULL,           -- layout and progression RNG
  persona       text        NOT NULL,           -- economy | towers | army
  level         integer     NOT NULL,           -- calculateBaseLevel, kept by the brain
  level_since   timestamptz NOT NULL,           -- when it reached this level (pace)
  state         text        NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active',  -- active | retired
  created_at    timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  retired_at    timestamptz
);
CREATE INDEX bot_state_level ON bym.bot (state, level);

CREATE TABLE bym.bot_job (
  id            bigserial   PRIMARY KEY,
  bot_userid    integer     NOT NULL REFERENCES bym.bot(userid),
  kind          text        NOT NULL,           -- grow | repair | revenge | declineTruce
  target_userid integer,                        -- revenge, declineTruce
  due_at        timestamptz NOT NULL,
  giveup_at     timestamptz,                    -- revenge: trigger + 72 h
  attempts      integer     NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  payload       jsonb       NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}',
  created_at    timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);
CREATE INDEX bot_job_due ON bym.bot_job (due_at);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX bot_job_one_grow   ON bym.bot_job (bot_userid) WHERE kind = 'grow';
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX bot_job_one_repair ON bym.bot_job (bot_userid) WHERE kind = 'repair';
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX bot_job_one_revenge ON bym.bot_job (bot_userid, target_userid) WHERE kind = 'revenge';
```

- **The flag lives in its own table**, never on `user` or `save`, so no existing query, field list
  or `@FrontendKey` serialisation (`save.model.ts`) can carry it to a client. Server code asks
  `isBot(userid)` (one indexed lookup, cached per request).
- Entities `bot.model.ts` and `botjob.model.ts`, added to entity discovery
  (`database/entityDiscovery.test.ts`).
- **`user.last_seen_at`** (decision 20): `timestamptz NULL`, set by the owner's own yard load
  (`baseLoad.ts`, next to the last-seen key at `:104`) at most once an hour, indexed for the
  neighbour search. Existing rows start null (not seen), so on live they count again from their
  next login. It is never sent to a client.
- No change to `save` or `maproom`. Revenge caps (7.2) are counted from `attack_logs`
  (`attacker_userid`, `attacktime`) joined to `bot`, so no counter table is needed.

## 6. Server jobs

**One sweep, in process,** like the finaliser: `startBotSweep()` in `server.ts`, every 60 seconds,
off unless `BOTS_BRAIN=on`. Each pass claims due jobs with

```sql
SELECT … FROM bym.bot_job WHERE due_at <= now() ORDER BY due_at LIMIT 20 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
```

inside a transaction, so two server processes never run the same job and a crash leaves the job
for the next pass. Jobs are rows, so they survive restarts. Each job reschedules itself or deletes
its row in the same transaction as its effect. A job that throws gets `attempts + 1` and a backoff;
after 5 attempts it is dropped with an error log.

| Job | When | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `grow` | every 2-6 h per bot | catch-up, progression forward to the pace target, resource band, champion fed, 2-minute presence, `bot.level` updated; retires the bot past level 40 |
| `repair` | 1-4 h after an attack | `planRepair` all, bunkers and Housing refilled, resource band |
| `revenge` | 1-24 h after an attack, 1 in 3 | section 4.7 |
| `declineTruce` | 2-8 h after a request | answer "rejected" |

**Rebalance is not a `bot_job` row.** It belongs to no bot, and `bot_job.bot_userid` is required.
Once a day the sweep claims the day in `bym.job_run` (`job = 'bots-rebalance'`, `period` = the UTC
date), the same way `scripts/monthly-shiny.ts` claims its month. The insert's primary key means
only one server runs it each day. It counts bots per level, nudges the pace and tops the total back
to `BOTS_TOTAL` (creating bots).

Every yard write runs under the save's row lock (`lockRow`, as `catchUpLockedYard`), and is skipped
while `isAttackActive` (an attack owns the row then), retrying in 10 minutes.

Load: 500 bots × one grow every ~4 h ≈ 125 grow jobs an hour, each one row read, a pure catch-up and
one write. Revenge battles are the only heavy step and share the replay-worker slots with manual
saves.

As built (WP7, `services/bots/sweep.ts` for the rows and the timer, `services/bots/brain.ts` for the
pure decisions): `server.ts` loads the sweep only when `BOTS_BRAIN` is on, so with it off nothing of
it runs. Each pass first books a `grow` (due within the hour) for every active bot without one, which
is how a bot made by the factory starts; its first grow's catch-up gives it mushrooms and the rest of a
real save. Jobs are claimed one at a time (`LIMIT 1 … SKIP LOCKED`, at most 20 a pass), each effect in
a savepoint so a throw undoes it and leaves the backoff (5, 10, 20, 40 minutes; dropped at the fifth
failure, and a dropped `grow` is booked afresh on the next pass). `grow` and `repair` are claimed, and
`revenge` while `BOTS_REVENGE` is on (WP11, §4.7), and `declineTruce` (WP12, §4.9). Details that the
text above leaves open:

- **Growth is a diff** of the live yard against the generator's yard at the pace target: missing ids
  are built, lower levels upgraded, each step earning its build or upgrade points; the step with the
  highest id is left on its countdown (capped at 90% of the time to the next grow). A mushroom on a
  new spot is picked; anything else in the way (or an id holding another type) refuses that growth
  whole and logs it. A damaged yard does not grow; the grow books a repair if none is booked.
- **The pace** is `level + days on it × speed / BOTS_DAYS_PER_LEVEL`; `level_since` is re-anchored when
  the level changes so a bot that could not grow catches up rather than losing time. The rebalance's
  nudges (`[PLACEHOLDER]` 0.75 slow, 1.5 fast) live in the `grow` job's `payload.speed` and end at the
  bot's next level. "Its arrivals sped up" for a thin level is read as the bots next to arrive: the
  furthest-on bots of the level below, as many as the level is short.
- **Retirement** happens when the pace position passes 41 (the bot has had its three days on level 40);
  the replacement level 1 bot is made after the retirement commits. The daily top-up adds level 1
  bots only up to level 1's share plus the slack of 2, so an empty table is never refilled at level 1
  all at once (use `bots.ts create --fill`).
- **Repair** also re-arms fired traps under their own ids (from `firedtraps`), refills bunkers and
  Housing that are under their band's floor (to the generator's garrison and army, cut to the Housing
  standing), feeds and heals the champion and puts loot back in its band.

## 7. Safety, anti-abuse and not leaking bots

### 7.1 Never attack the wrong player

- Revenge targets only the attacker of that bot, only on Map Room 1, only through
  `baseModeAttack`'s own refusals (protected, online, under attack, truce), checked again at run
  time, never cached.
- Protection includes new-player protection, so a brand-new player who has not attacked is never
  touched. A new player who attacks a bot loses their protection by the normal rule
  (`damageProtection.ts:42-48`), which is what makes revenge possible at all.
- Bots never attack first, never attack bots, never attack outposts or Map Room 2/3.
- One revenge in flight per bot (a per-bot Redis lock as `auto-attack:<userid>`), and the unique
  index allows one pending revenge per bot and player.

### 7.2 Revenge caps (decision 19)

At most **2 revenge attacks on one player per 24 hours** and **1 per bot-player pair per 24
hours**, counted from `attack_logs`. A roll that would break a cap
is simply not scheduled. A global switch `BOTS_REVENGE=off` stops new revenge and cancels pending
jobs.

### 7.3 Abuse by players

- **Farming bots:** bots are worth what a real yard of that level is worth and are protected after
  a heavy attack like anyone. The 10-attacks-a-day rule drops a pair from each other's lists
  (`registerAttacker.ts:77-85`).
- **Logging in as a bot:** login and password reset refuse bot accounts; the email domain is
  `.invalid`.
- **Spotting bots:** see 7.4. A player who spots one gains nothing beyond knowing.

### 7.4 Not leaking (decision 3)

| Leak | Guard |
| --- | --- |
| A field or flag in a response | The flag is in `bot`, read by name only. WP12 tests that every response a player can get about a bot (attack and view loads, neighbours, Map Room 1 read, mail targets, attack logs) has exactly the shape a real player's has. |
| Every bot made the same minute | `createtime` back-dated by level; live creation spread over a few days (section 10). |
| Never online | Short presence on grow jobs (4.9). |
| Only bots send defence reports or replays | Reports (and later replays) for every Map Room 1 main-yard defence (4.8). |
| Identical yards | Seeded progression, personas, jittered placement, banded resources. |
| Bot names | Word lists in several real styles, unique, filtered. |
| Unanswered messages | Real players often do not answer either. |

As built (WP12): the audit runs the real routes for a bot fresh from the factory and for a player of
the same level who signed up, loaded their yard and opened Map Room 1 (`services/bots/leakAudit.db.test.ts`,
opt-in on a throwaway database): view load, attack load, Map Room 1 read, `bm/neighbours/get`, mail
targets, thread list and attack logs have the same fields holding the same kinds of value
(`testing/shapeDiff.ts`), and no response carries an email or a bot-table field. A pure test compares a
bot's client-visible save at every level with a real account's (`lookAlike.test.ts`). It found two
differences, both fixed: a bot made but not yet grown lacked what a loaded yard carries (the hatchery
queues in `monsters`, mushrooms, harvester cycles), so the factory now catches each new save up to its
own `savetime` (`settleNewYard`); and a bot's `wmstatus` was empty where a player who has opened Map Room
1 has four tribes, so the factory and every grow write them as opening the map does (`lookAlike.ts`,
sharing `mr1TribeStatuses` with `createMR1Tribes`). Left as they are (values, not shapes): every bot keeps
the new-save Shiny (`credits`), which the view load shows; bots made in one run have consecutive user and
base ids; a bot never reads plain messages, so its unread count only grows; its tribes are never marked
wrecked; and it buys nothing from the store beyond expansions.

### 7.5 Scale

With 36 hours of protection after each heavy attack, one bot absorbs about one heavy attack every
day and a half, so 500 bots absorb roughly 300 heavy attacks a day across all levels, about 12 per
level. That is plenty for a small player base and thin if hundreds of new players arrive in one
week. The bot count and the pace are config values (`BOTS_TOTAL`, `BOTS_DAYS_PER_LEVEL`), so they
can be raised without code.

## 8. What the client needs

Nothing visible about bots. Two small web changes the design depends on:

1. **Presence ping (needed before revenge goes live).** While the tab is visible and the player is in
   their own yard or the map, `POST /bm/presence` every 30 seconds; the server route only refreshes
   `last-seen:main:<userid>` (120 s, as `baseLoad.ts:104`). Without it, an idle web player reads as
   offline and a revenge (or a real attack) can land while they watch.
2. **The defence notice kind** (`yardAttacked`) in the away toast (`web/src/ui/yard/JobNotices.ts:152`)
   and as a notice in the mailbox, with no "Show on map" (Map Room 1 has no cells).

Later (v1.1): "Watch" on a defence notice, reusing the attack scene's playback mode.

## 9. Testing

**Unit (bun, beside the source):**

- Generator: for every level 1-40 and 20 seeds each, `calculateBaseLevel` of the result equals the
  target; every type within `quantity[hall]` and `re`; no overlaps and inside the plot; Map Room at
  level 1; bunkers and Housing within capacity; only unlocked monsters; resources inside the band.
  Same seed and points give the same yard.
- Progression: a yard at points P is a prefix of the yard at P' > P (growth never moves or removes
  a building).
- Neighbour search: real players seen in the last 30 days fill first, others never; bots fill the rest within ±7; a real player outside the
  150 most recent saves is found; retired bots never appear; counters carried over on re-search;
  the attackable-count retry.
- Revenge decision: roll rate with a seeded RNG; caps; postponement and give-up; refused for
  protected, online, under attack, truce, off Map Room 1, retired bot.
- Revenge plan: drop points clear of buildings and inside the bucket cap; replayed in the shared
  engine against generated yards of several levels, it does damage and never errors.
- Revenge landing: equal to a manual save of the same log and seed (as `autoAttack.test.ts` checks);
  a replay timeout leaves nothing spent and the sweep lands it exactly once.
- Jobs: `SKIP LOCKED` claim by two runners never runs one job twice; a throwing job backs off.
- Seed: `DEV_SANDBOX=true` seed gives a starter yard.
- Leak: response shapes for a bot equal those for a real player (7.4).

**Dev end to end:** create 500 bots on the dev database (after a snapshot), turn the switches on,
check the level histogram, open a fresh account's Map Room 1 list, attack a bot, check repair and
refill after the delay, force a revenge with a test hook (`BOTS_REVENGE_DELAY=60`) on a test
account, and read the notice and the attack log.

## 10. Rollout

Three switches in `server/.env`, all off by default: `BOTS_FILL` (neighbour fill), `BOTS_BRAIN`
(the sweep: grow, repair, rebalance) and `BOTS_REVENGE`.

**Dev:** snapshot `bym`, run the migration, run the seed fix, then
`bun src/scripts/bots.ts create --target 500`, switch on all three.

**Live:**

1. Ship the neighbour rewrite, the defence notice and the presence ping first, switches off. The
   rewritten search excludes bots, so it can go out before any bot exists.
2. Back up the database. Run the migration.
3. `bun src/scripts/bots.ts create --target 500 --per-day 170`: creates the low levels first, in
   batches of 25 per transaction, idempotent (it counts active bots and makes only the difference),
   `--dry-run` prints the plan. Bots are invisible until `BOTS_FILL` is on.
4. Switch on `BOTS_BRAIN`, then `BOTS_FILL`. Watch for a week: list sizes, attacks on bots,
   protection rates.
5. Switch on `BOTS_REVENGE`.

**Kill switch:** `bots.ts retire-all` moves every bot off Map Room 1 at once; every list drops them
on its next read. `bots.ts delete-retired` removes retired bots and their jobs (only when nothing in
the mail references them).

As built (WP6, `services/bots/factory.ts`, `scripts/bots.ts`): `create` also takes `--fill` (up to
`BOTS_TOTAL`), `--count N` (the next N of a fill) and `--levels 5,15,25 [--count N]` (a few bots
on chosen levels, for testing), and `--seed N` for repeatable draws. `--per-day N` counts the bots
made in the last 24 hours, so a daily run spreads the rollout. For dev only, `remove-all --yes`
(refused when `ENV` is production) deletes every bot with its mail, truces and attack logs. The
factory writes no `grow` job: the sweep (WP7) books each active bot's first one. Avatars are a
critter for 65% of bots and the sign-up placeholder for the rest `[PLACEHOLDER]`.

## 12. Decisions that turned out costly

None is impossible. Three cost more than they look:

- **Decision 13, replay.** No defender replay exists. Offering one for revenge alone would mark the
  attacker as a bot (decision 3), so it has to come for every Map Room 1 main-yard defence. Decided
  as v1.1 (decision 21, WP13, M), with the mail report in v1. The report has the same catch and is small (WP8).
- **Decision 13, "while the player is away".** The web client never reports presence, so "away" is
  wrong after one idle minute. A small fix (WP9), but revenge must not go live without it.
- **Decision 5 with 36-hour protection.** Bots protected after heavy attacks can leave a two-week
  list with nothing to attack. The attackable-count retry (4.3) handles it; at a large player count
  500 bots run thin (7.5).
